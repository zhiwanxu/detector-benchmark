Particle Reconstruction Benchmark Score

One score for the whole reconstruction chain.

PRBS grades a trained model against fixed physics targets on clustering, energy, position and particle identification. The four benchmarks carry equal weight, every metric is capped at its target, and every plot uses one standard binning so results from different groups can be overlaid directly.

\[ S_{\mathrm{PRBS}} = \tfrac{1}{4}\left(S_{\mathrm{clu}} + S_{E} + S_{\mathrm{pos}} + S_{\mathrm{cls}}\right) \]

Four scoring rules cover every metric

Each measured value \(x\) is turned into a 0–100 score. Where a target \(t\) exists, meeting it gives 100 and nothing extra is earned beyond it. Every percentage below is a share of the benchmark's own 100 points.

Higher is better

\[ \Sup{x}{t} = 100\,\min\!\left(1,\tfrac{x}{t}\right) \]

efficiency, purity, accuracy, background rejection

Lower is better

\[ \Sdn{x}{t} = 100\,\min\!\left(1,\tfrac{t}{x}\right) \]

resolution σ or B, position |μ|, split and merge rates

Rejection factor

\[ \Slog{R}{R^{t}} = 100\,\min\!\left(1,\tfrac{\log_{10}R}{\log_{10}R^{t}}\right) \]

π, non-EM and single-γ rejection; log scale because R spans decades

Distance from ideal 0

\[ \Szero{x} = 100\,\max\!\left(0,\,1-|x|\right) \]

energy bias μ (−1 scores 0), cluster-count deviation

Four benchmarks, one quarter each

Every card follows the same order: what the quantity is, which samples are used, how the benchmark's 100 points are split, and the score formula. The DIS sample is always one sample: charged-current e + p at 10 × 100 GeV with 100 < Q² < 1000 GeV². The four particle-ID cases are EM (e⁻, e⁺, γ), non-EM (all other particles), γ only and electron only.

BENCHMARK 2

Energy reconstruction

25 % of PRBS
Definition
  • For every reconstructed cluster matched to a truth particle, the relative residual is \(r = (E_{\mathrm{reco}}-E_{\mathrm{true}})/E_{\mathrm{true}}\).
  • Energy resolution \(\sigma_E/E\) is the width of the \(r\) distribution in a bin (σ₆₈, half-width of its central 68.3 %). The bias \(\mu\) is its centre (median); \(\mu=0\) is unbiased, \(\mu=-1\) means no energy reconstructed.
  • The resolution versus energy follows \(\sigma_E/E = A + B/\sqrt{E} + C/E\): \(B\) is the stochastic term, \(A\) the constant term, \(C\) the noise term.
Datasets
Multi-particle γ + e⁻ 20 % Single γ 10 % Single e⁻ 10 % DIS · CC e + p, 10 × 100 GeV, Q² 100–1000 GeV² 60 %
Metric definitions
Stochastic term \(B\)
from the fit of \(\sigma_E/E\) over the 10 energy bins. Design requirement \(B \le 11\,\%/\sqrt{E}\); above is penalised, lower is better and is reported, the score is capped at 100.
η-bin resolution \(B_k\)
no fit in η, since the η dependence has no expected shape (it may be flat). In each η bin \(k\), \(B_k = \sigma_{68}\big(r\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{true}}}\big)\): the resolution scaled to 1 GeV, so it is compared directly with the same 11 % design value
Bias \(\mu_b\)
median of \(r\) in bin \(b\); expected to grow at high energy, so it is checked in every bin
Efficiency \(\varepsilon_E\)
fraction of true deposited energy contained in matched clusters
Purity \(\pi_E\)
fraction of reconstructed cluster energy that belongs to the matched particle
Background rejection \(R_E\)
fraction of background (noise) energy kept out of clusters
Accuracy \(a_E\)
fraction of matched clusters with \(|r|\) inside a frozen window \(\delta\) (TBD)

BENCHMARK 3

Position reconstruction

25 % of PRBS
Definition
  • Position reconstruction estimates where a particle hits the calorimeter and its direction, from the cluster.
  • For each matched cluster and coordinate \(q\in\{x,y,z,\eta,\phi\}\), the residual is \(\Delta q = q_{\mathrm{reco}}-q_{\mathrm{true}}\), with the truth point at the calorimeter impact point and Δφ wrapped into [−π, π].
  • Position resolution \(\sigma_q\) is the width of Δq in a bin (σ₆₈); position bias \(\mu_q\) is its centre (median).

BENCHMARK 4

Particle classification

25 % of PRBS
Definition
  • Classification assigns each cluster a particle species. PRBS scores three binary separations, each with a signal class S and a background class B: EM vs non-EM (S = EM), e⁻ vs π⁻ (S = e⁻), π⁰ vs γ (S = π⁰, so the π⁰ sample is as pure as possible).
  • A rejection factor \(R = N_{B}/N_{B\to S}\) is the number of background clusters divided by those mis-identified as signal, at a fixed signal efficiency of 95 %. \(R = 10^{2}\) means 1 % of the background leaks into the signal sample.

Standard binning and plotting

Every group draws every plot in the same bins, so curves from different models overlay without rebinning. The only fit anywhere is the energy-resolution fit \(\sigma_E/E = A + B/\sqrt{E} + C/E\); its A, B, C summarise the shape of the curve. Every other quantity is quoted, and scored, bin by bin.

How single-particle and DIS samples are weighted

Single-particle samples test the intrinsic response with clean truth. DIS is the physics environment the detector is built for, so it carries the larger share wherever both exist.

Acceptance gates and grades

The score ranks models. The gates decide whether a model is acceptable at all; a model failing any gate is labelled FAIL regardless of its PRBS.

  • G1Inclusive e⁻/π⁻: pion rejection \(R_{\pi}\ge 10^{2}\) at 95 % electron efficiency.
  • G2Every benchmark scores at least 60 on its own.
